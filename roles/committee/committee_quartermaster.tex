\section{Role Overview}
The Quartermaster supports the youth Troop Quartermaster by purchasing and maintaining equipment beyond what a Scout should be responsible for, managing the adult leaders' patrol box, and keeping major gear like tents, stoves, and the trailer in working order. Day-to-day patrol equipment and inventories stay with the Troop Quartermaster.

\vspace{0.4em}
\section{Responsibilities}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Train and support the Troop Quartermaster in maintaining patrol equipment and inventories
  \item Purchase, maintain, and store major troop equipment (tents, stoves, trailer) beyond Scout-level upkeep
  \item Manage and stock the adult leaders' patrol box
  \item Inspect major equipment after outings and coordinate repairs or replacement
  \item Coordinate with the Treasurer on the equipment budget and purchases
  \item Coordinate with the Outdoor/Activities Chair on equipment needs for upcoming outings
\end{itemize}

\section{Training Requirements}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Required:} Current Safeguarding Youth Training (SYT)
  \item \textbf{Required:} Scouts BSA - Troop Committee Position-Specific Training (my.scouting.org)
\end{itemize}

\section{Notes \& Resources}
Keep the troop-level equipment inventory and purchase records in a shared location accessible to the Scoutmaster and Outdoor/Activities Chair. The outgoing Quartermaster should hand off that inventory, trailer and storage access, and any pending repair or replacement needs during transition. This is a functional role: a registered committee member is preferred, but it may be filled by program leadership if needed.
